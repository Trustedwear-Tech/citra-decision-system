Open Source

Three of the engines behind Citra are published under Apache-2.0. Clone them, read them, run them on your own infrastructure against your own models — free, permanently, with no seat count and no call with us first.

The repositories

What is published

citra-decision-system

The decision engine and the Decision Apps runtime. Describe a decision in plain English and it produces an app that recommends the call, acts only inside the policy gate you set, and records the reasoning behind every approve and reject so the next recommendation is better. Includes the MCP tool gateway, the discovery service and the sandboxed builder.

github.com/Trustedwear-Tech/citra-decision-system

citra-decks

Presentations, visual reports and long-form documents written from your own material — the spec, the spreadsheets, last quarter's report. Figures are computed by running code against your actual files rather than written by a model, so every number traces back to a source.

github.com/Trustedwear-Tech/citra-decks

citra-flows

AI-authored workflow automation. Describe a pipeline in plain English; the builder assembles it from typed nodes you can open, inspect and edit on a canvas — it cannot invent a node. Run it, watch every input and output, then deploy it behind a webhook or a cron schedule.

github.com/Trustedwear-Tech/citra-flows

Run it yourself

A complete deployment, not a trial tier

Each repository ships a quickstart wizard that writes its own .env with fresh secrets and brings the stack up with Docker Compose. Point it at an OpenRouter key or at a vLLM endpoint you run, and no data leaves your network. The models are open-weight by default.

No licence key, no seat count, and no telemetry back to us. The decision engine, the builder, the governed ontology, the decision ledger, the memory curation UI and the automation controls are all here — you can see what the system has learned, retire a judgement, and stop every automated job, because a decision system you cannot inspect or switch off is not one anybody should run. Apache-2.0 means you may run it in production, modify it, and never speak to us at all.

There is also a commercial edition. It adds nothing to the decision-making and takes nothing away from this one — it is the operational layer an institution needs once a deployment is load-bearing and there is more than one of them: judgement memory managed rather than curated by hand, HA and sharded retrieval, SSO and fine-grained RBAC, encryption with your own KMS, regulator-ready audit packs, point-in-time restore. We would rather say so here than have you find out later.

Stuck getting it running, or want to argue with a design decision? Join the Discord — one server for all three projects.

Why we publish it

Sovereignty you can verify

Citra is sold to government, PSU and BFSI teams on the claim that their data stays inside their estate. That claim is worth more when the code can be read. Publishing the engines means a security reviewer can check what leaves the network instead of taking a vendor's word, and a customer is never locked to us by the software itself — only by whether the hosting and support are worth paying for.
