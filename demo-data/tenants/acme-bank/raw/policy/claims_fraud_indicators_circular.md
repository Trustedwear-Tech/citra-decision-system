# Claims Fraud Indicators — Circular AB/CLM/2026-04

**Document type:** Circular · **Owner:** Claims Investigation Unit
**Classification:** Internal · **Issued:** 15 April 2026

> Synthetic circular written for the Acme Bank demo tenant.

---

## 1. Purpose

To list the indicators that warrant investigation before a claim is settled,
and to state plainly what an indicator is and is not.

**An indicator is a reason to look harder. It is not a finding of fraud, and it
is never, by itself, a ground for repudiation.** A claim is repudiated on a
policy clause and evidence, not on suspicion. Every claim carries a customer
who may simply have had a bad year.

## 2. Documentary indicators

- **The same document appearing on two claims.** A repair estimate, invoice or
  photograph that is byte-for-byte identical to one already on file against a
  different claim. Genuine duplication happens (a workshop reissuing the same
  estimate for the same vehicle), so establish which claim the file legitimately
  belongs to before concluding anything.
- A photograph whose visible subject does not match the insured asset —
  a different registration number, a different model, or a schematic rather
  than a photograph of the actual damage.
- Invoices from an entity with no verifiable registration, or serially
  numbered invoices issued out of date order.
- A discharge summary without the hospital's stamp, or bearing a doctor's
  registration number that does not resolve.
- Documents dated **after** the claim was intimated but describing events
  before it, with no explanation.

## 3. Timing indicators

- Loss occurring within 15 days of policy inception, or within 15 days of an
  increase in sum insured.
- Loss occurring within days of a lapse being regularised.
- Intimation delayed well beyond the policy window with no supporting reason.
- A report or estimate **generated after the inspection date it describes**.

## 4. Pattern indicators

- More than two claims by the same insured in 12 months across any line.
- Multiple claims sharing an identifier — the same mobile number, bank account,
  garage, hospital, or surveyor — across otherwise unrelated insureds.
- Claims clustering around one agent or one branch beyond the portfolio norm.
- A total-loss claim on a vehicle with an inspection due or recently failed.

## 5. Behavioural indicators

- Pressure for immediate settlement, disproportionate to the amount.
- Reluctance to allow a survey or an inspection.
- Inconsistent accounts of the incident between the claim form, the FIR and
  the surveyor's record.
- Willingness to accept a markedly reduced settlement without discussion.

## 6. What to do when an indicator fires

1. **Do not accuse.** Do not tell the customer they are suspected.
2. Record the indicator and the evidence for it on the claim.
3. Route to the Claims Investigation Unit where **two or more** indicators fire,
   or where any documentary indicator in §2 fires on its own.
4. Continue the normal claims process meanwhile — an investigation does not
   suspend the service TAT unless the CIU says so in writing.
5. Where investigation clears the claim, settle it and record that it was
   cleared. A cleared indicator is useful information, not an embarrassment.

## 7. Recording outcomes

Every investigation closes with one of: `substantiated`, `not_substantiated`,
or `inconclusive`. All three are recorded. A unit that only records
substantiated cases cannot tell how often it is wrong, and neither can anyone
reviewing its work.

## 8. A note on judgement

This list is not exhaustive and will never be. Experienced officers see
combinations no circular anticipates — a story that does not fit, a document
that is technically in order but wrong for the circumstances. Where you act on
such a judgement, **write down what you saw**. That record is how the next
officer learns what this circular has not yet caught up with.
